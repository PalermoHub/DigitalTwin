# Civil Protection emergency plan

## The «Emergency plan» layer

Keywords: emergency plan, civil protection, waiting area, shelter area, reception area, assembly area, rescuers, emergency, evacuation, meeting point, earthquake, flood.

Typical questions: Where should I go in case of an earthquake? Which is the waiting area nearest to my home? Where are the reception areas? What do green, red and yellow mean?

In the Layers panel, in the **«Events»** group, **«Emergency plan»** section, the **«Emergency plan (from zoom 11)»** layer shows the areas of the Palermo Civil Protection emergency plan, with the symbology of **Annex B** of the Civil Protection Department (**hatched** areas with a grey outline and, at the centre, the class's **blue symbol**), coloured by function:

- **green**: **waiting area for the population**, where people gather right after the event;
- **red**: **shelter and reception area**, where people are housed;
- **yellow**: **rescuers' assembly area** (including the Fire Brigade mobile column);
- **blue** and **purple**, for context: **hospitals** and **industrial risk sites**.

The legend, under the layer, shows how many areas there are of each type and lets you turn them on or off one by one. Areas appear from **zoom 11**.

## How to reach an area in an emergency

Keywords: my location, where am I, reach an area, nearest area, route, directions, navigation, smartphone, phone, emergency, evacuation, how to get there.

Typical questions: How do I see where I am relative to the areas? How do I get to the nearest emergency area? How do I find the way to the waiting area? How can my phone guide me to a Civil Protection area? What should I do in an earthquake or flood to know where to go?

In an emergency, from a phone, six steps are enough:

1. **Tap «Emergencies»** on the home page. Civil Protection bulletin notifications arrive here: when there is an alert in Palermo the button **turns red** and the subtitle says which one (for example «Yellow alert in Palermo: see the bulletin»); with no alert it stays white with a red border.
2. **Read the bulletin** at the top of the panel: for each zone it gives the **type of warning** (yellow, orange or red alert, with the risks: thunderstorms, hydrogeological, hydraulic), with the bulletin date and time. The **«Bulletin on the Civil Protection website»** link opens the official site directly. Then, on the **«Emergency plan»** card, tap **«Add to map»**: the bottom bar turns red (emergency mode).
3. **Look at the map**: the areas appear coloured by function. With **«GPS»** (allow access to your location) a dot shows where you are, so you can see which area is nearest.
4. **Zoom the map in** on the nearest area: colour and hatching tell you what it is for.
5. **Tap the area**, on the hatched polygon or on its blue symbol: the card of that area opens, with number, surface and where it is. **Tap the green «Open in navigator» button** (the «Percorso» row): your smartphone's navigation app opens with directions already set to that area. Start the route and follow the directions.
6. **Scroll the card** for the **useful numbers** (112, 115, 118, 113, 1515, Civil Protection), the official websites and the map with the chosen area in pink.

## The «Emergencies» button and the Civil Protection bulletin

Keywords: Emergencies button, bulletin, alert, yellow alert, orange, red, warning, notification, civil protection, red button, bulletin link, criticality.

Typical questions: Why is the Emergencies button red? Where do I read what kind of alert there is in Palermo? How do I open the Civil Protection bulletin? How do warnings arrive?

The home page **«Emergencies»** button receives the **Civil Protection criticality bulletin notifications** (Department of Civil Protection, Sicilian alert zones). When there is an alert for Palermo the button **turns red** and the subtitle shows the level. Tapping it opens the panel, where you read **the type of warning** for each zone (level and risks) with the bulletin date and time, and can open the **direct link to the Civil Protection website** («Bulletin on the Civil Protection website»). The same panel holds the **Emergency plan** card («Add to map» button) and the useful numbers.

To know what each area is for, look at its colour: **green** waiting area, **red** shelter and reception, **yellow** rescuers' assembly. In an emergency, always follow the instructions of Civil Protection and the authorities.

## The profile of an area

Keywords: emergency area profile, area number, surface, district, neighbourhood, UPL.

Typical questions: In which neighbourhood is the waiting area? How big is it? How do I find the nearest area to an address?

A click on the map, **even with the layer off**, shows in the **Place** tab the areas containing the clicked point: title (the area's function), **area number**, **type**, **surface** (in m² or hectares), any notes and **where it is**: **district** (circoscrizione), **neighbourhood** and **UPL**. If several areas overlap, the profile gathers them in one accordion entry. The map popup summarises title, zone and surface.

At the bottom of the section there is a **small map of the emergency areas** (move it with two fingers on a phone, Ctrl + scroll on a computer, or the + and − buttons): the areas of the Plan are coloured by type and the one you tapped is highlighted in pink, with a marker. It shows at a glance where the area is compared with the others nearby.

The data are also in the **data table** (with district, neighbourhood and UPL columns, handy for filtering and exporting).

## Emergency mode on the phone

Keywords: emergency mode, phone, smartphone, simplify, area only, Talia, exit, bottom bar.

Typical questions: Why do I only have Map and Talia on the phone? How do I leave emergency mode? How do I get all the layers back?

On the **phone**, when the «Emergency Plan» layer is on (alone or with the «Emergency areas» route), the app is reduced to the essentials: the bottom bar has only **Map**, **GPS**, **Share**, **Talia** and **Exit**, the map tools disappear (GPS is in the bar), the layers sheet does not open, and a tap on an area opens **only that area's card**, without the other information for the point.

A tap does not open the map popup (the tooltip stays) and the card opens at full height.

The **Map** tab closes the card and takes you back to the map.

To **leave**, tap **Exit**, the last tab of the bar: the full bar, the layers and the full cards come back, and the layer stays on. Turning the layer off and on again restarts the mode. It also works when you **rotate the phone**: in landscape the red bar stays at the bottom and the area card opens on the right. It does not apply on large screens (computers, tablets).

## Useful numbers and official websites

Keywords: useful numbers, emergency number, 112, 115, 118, 113, 1515, 800 840 840, 800 404 040, phone, call, fire brigade, ambulance, police, civil protection, operations room, official websites, what to tell the operator.

Typical questions: Which number do I call in an emergency? What is the Civil Protection number? How do I call the fire brigade? What should I say when I call? Where are the official Civil Protection websites? Can I call from the card?

Below the area and the navigation button, the card lists **emergency numbers**: **112** (single number), **115** (fire brigade), **118** (medical emergency), **113** (State Police), **1515** (forest fires), the national civil protection toll-free number **800 840 840** and the Sicily Region operations room **800 404 040**. On a phone, tap a number to open the dialer with the number already entered (on a computer the numbers are read-only). A reminder says what to tell the operator: where you are, what happened, how many people are involved, and do not hang up until told to.

Official websites («Official websites» rows at the end of the card section): national Civil Protection (https://www.protezionecivile.gov.it/it/), Sicily Region Civil Protection (https://www.protezionecivilesicilia.it/it/) and Civil Protection of the Municipality of Palermo (https://protezionecivile.comune.palermo.it/). If in doubt, in a real emergency call **112**: it is free and routes the request to the right service.

## Source and limits

Keywords: source, licence, Open Data Portal, update, dataset 351, reliability.

Typical questions: Where do the data come from? Are they up to date? Can I trust the areas?

Source: Municipality of Palermo, Civil Protection, **Emergency plan** (Open Data Portal, dataset 351, CC BY 4.0 licence), published in 2015 and updated in 2021; district, neighbourhood and UPL were added by DigitalTwin Palermo by crossing the areas with the census sections. **The data are informative**: in an emergency follow the instructions of Civil Protection and the authorities; areas may have changed since the last update.
